## Inspiration

Visual art exists for the eye.

But for **295 million people** worldwide, that experience is out of reach due to severe vision impairment.

From the Louvre to the Tate Modern, blind visitors drift helplessly through art galleries: dragged around by companions, unable to trace the winding contours of a Renaissance fresco for themselves. Art galleries have historically offered **almost no services** for blind visitors. **After all, how could a blind viewer experience a visual-medium artwork... or can they?**

## What MonaListen does

Picture a landscape painting of a stream at the foot of a mountain. MonaListen gives artists a visual editor to draw regions, attach generated sound effects, and see live edits to falloff curves and blend radius. Using a live link, a blind visitor points their phone at the painting, moves their hand up/down in space, and hears each sound grow in volume within the mix as the corresponding region is approached.

A **visual artist** uploads images of their artwork, then uses an editor to place point emitters or draw spline-bounded audio regions across the piece. For each region, they write a text prompt to generate a looping sound effect tied to that part of the painting: from the steady rush of the river to the turbulent wind at the mountaintop. As a music producer myself, I designed the sound controls with care. Artists can spatially configure how each sound blends with distance, choose a falloff curve, and pan either left/right with a simple slider. The finished soundscape is saved with a live link for viewers to use.

A friend helps the **blind viewer** open the shared link on their phone. Spoken instructions guide their hand until the phone camera intersects the painting, entering the sound landscape the artist built: with a pair of earbuds on, a blind viewer simply moves their phone across the artwork to traverse its coordinate space: exploring the sounds of a living, spatial scene entirely by ear.
